Fable Learning Traverse: a tighter corridor

The follow-up to the Opus Pilot Traverse. I moved the stubs, re-drew my duct, hid it, and this time trimmed the pipes to stop just shy of it, so the corridor is only an inch or two wider than the duct. Claude Fable 5.1, carrying the tooling and lessons from the pilot, had to get through on real SA02 fittings, including the half-throat gored elbows I used, without ever seeing my route.

How it went

What changed from the pilot

  • Stubs moved; 387 pipe segments instead of 313.
  • Pipes trimmed to stop just short of my duct, so the free corridor is 1 to 2 inches wider than a 16" duct.
  • Non-standard bend angles, including an 89 degree elbow, and half-throat gored elbows.
  • Claude worked from its own survey of the model and a parts list I allowed: fitting types and sizes only, no positions.

The approach

Claude surveyed the lattice, found the widest corridor, and optimised corner positions against an exact model of the 5-gore elbow measured from Revit's own meshes. The fixed 23.8" elbow radius couldn't make the bends, and that cost most of the day until the parts list showed half-throat elbows. The Revit API reports that option as locked; it turned out to live in the palette button's condition. Straights were placed exactly and elbows rolled into plane numerically, then every joint was closed and checked on the real fabrication geometry.

The result

  • A Square to Round, then 16" round all the way: gored elbows at non-standard angles, several at half throat, and spiral straights.
  • Every joint closed within 0.014", landing on the north stub.
  • Tightest gap 0.51" on the real meshes. Zero contacts.
  • About 3.9 hours of AI work and 2 questions to me, against 20 questions in the pilot. Fewer fittings is the next target.

Revit 2024's built-in Interference Check doesn't support MEP Fabrication parts, so clearance was verified directly against the fabrication meshes. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Autodesk, Inc. or Anthropic.
