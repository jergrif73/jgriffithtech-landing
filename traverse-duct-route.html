AI coordination challenge · Revit 2024 + Claude

Threading a 16" duct through 313 pipes

I drew a duct between two wall stubs, hid it, filled the room with a lattice of 3" chilled water pipes, and deleted only the pipes the duct hit. Then I asked Claude, working in Revit through an MCP connection, to reconnect the stubs with real SA02 fabrication parts without touching a single pipe. It never got to see my duct.

How it went

The rules

  • Connect a 24×12 TDC stub to a 16" round stub on opposite walls.
  • Any fitting in the SA02 service, real installed geometry, flanges included.
  • Hold the free area of the 16" round and a 4:1 maximum aspect ratio.
  • TDC above 20", S&D or round connectors below.
  • Don't modify the stubs, stay inside the walls, and never look at the hidden workset.
  • Fewest fittings wins.

The approach

Claude surveyed the model first, then mapped every position a 16" duct centerline could occupy without touching a pipe. That exposed one shaft down through the lattice and a 0.3" dogleg that forces a long down-north slope. It solved for the corner points, dry-ran the parts in Revit, checked every mesh triangle against every pipe, and rolled back. The real build ran only after I approved the plan.

The result

  • Square to Round, three 5-gore elbows (64°, 76°, 62°), four 16" spiral straights.
  • All 18 connectors connected, closing on the north stub with zero gap.
  • Tightest gap 0.49", set by where the north stub sits.
  • About 20 minutes of AI work and 30 minutes of my answers across the session.

Revit 2024's built-in Interference Check doesn't support MEP Fabrication parts, so clearance was verified directly against the fabrication meshes. Not affiliated with or endorsed by Autodesk, Inc. or Anthropic.
